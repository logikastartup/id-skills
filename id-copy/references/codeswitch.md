# codeswitch — which words stay English

`component: SHARED` · `version: 1 (2026-10-31)`.
Rule of thumb: **absorbed loanwords and UI vocabulary stay English;
official "padanan" are for formal prose only; forcing them into casual or
marketing copy is itself a tell** (stiff, translated-feel).

## Stay English in speech registers (Jaksel/code-mix fillers)

`literally`, `which is`, `worth it`, `even` — natural in gaul/Jaksel-flavored
copy, WRONG in formal writing, and even casually they have grammar (Grid.id:
"literally" only when something literally happened; past events take
"which was" not "which is") — [Grid.id](https://www.grid.id/read/042306717/sering-dipakai-anak-gaul-jaksel-ternyata-begini-cara-tepat-pakai-kata-even-literally-dan-which-is?page=all).
A brand performing these without fluency is the cringe scenario — use at
register level 4+ only, sparingly.

## Reverse direction (watch for it)

Absorbed English-flavored forms have displaced native words even in neutral
prose: **destinasi** > tujuan, **selebrasi** > perayaan
([Tirto](https://tirto.id/benarkah-kita-semua-anak-jaksel-yang-tertunda-like-literally-cYhA)).
Don't "correct" these back — the absorbed form IS the living word.

## Official-term trap

Official padanan can die quietly: **"surel" is no longer in KBBI Daring**
(checked live) though old lists still cite it as the correct word for
email. Don't trust padanan lists on age — trust current usage.
