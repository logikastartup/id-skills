# register — the Indonesian marketing register ladder

`component: SHARED` · `version: 1 (2026-10-31)` · reused by later id-* skills
by versioned copy (`copied-from: id-copy@v1`).

Choose ONE level explicitly before drafting. Averaging levels is how copy
becomes "safe and cheesy". Every example below is real and sourced.

## The ladder

| # | Level | You write with | Right for | Wrong for | Real example (source) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Baku-formal** | Capital **Anda**; full KBBI (*senantiasa*, *melayani*); brand says *kami*; no particles | Banks, insurance, gov, corporate pages, T&C | Chat commerce, youth social — reads cold, distant | BCA — *"Senantiasa di Sisi Anda"* ([BCA identity manual](https://www.bca.co.id/-/media/Feature/Card/List-Card/Tentang-BCA/Brand-Assets/PDF/Corporate-Identity-Manual--CIM--BCA.pdf)) |
| 2 | **Semi-formal santai** | Anda/kamu mix; softeners *yuk, ayo*; marketing verbs (*dapatkan, nikmati, jelajahi*); sparse emoji | Content marketing, app transactional copy, promos | Slang chat contexts — still stiff there | Traveloka-style promo: *"Dapatkan diskon hingga 10% untuk trip kamu pakai kode …"* (no stable URL — pattern-level) |
| 3 | **Chat-santai** | **Kak** as address + *kamu* grammar; particle *ya*; verbs drop formality | Marketplace chat, WA shops, CS, live commerce | Formal docs, B2B email | *"Halo Kak 😊!"* / *"pesanan kamu sedang diproses"* ([Jubelio templates](https://jubelio.com/6-contoh-auto-reply-chat-shopee-bantu-tingkatkan-penjualan)); *"Halo, Kakak! Sedang cari apa nih?"* ([Kitalulus](https://www.kitalulus.com/blog/bisnis/contoh-pesan-otomatis-whatsapp-bisnis)) |
| 4 | **Gaul hybrid** | kamu/gue light mix; *bestie, kuy, nih, udah, banget*; wordplay; heavier emoji | Youth F&B, coffee, social-only content | Banks, elder-facing, trust-critical | NEO Coffee — *"Kopi GUE, NEO Coffee Moccachini, Cobain Kuy!"* + #BosenYangBiasa (viral video campaign; no indexable article — pattern-level) |
| 5 | **Full slang** | lo/gue pair, Betawi flavor (*rese*); aggressive informality | Mass youth TVCs, meme content only | Everything service- or trust-shaped | Snickers — *"Lo Rese Kalo Lagi Laper"* (TVC/video; no web article — pattern-level) |

## Decision rules (in order)

1. **Trust stakes** — high-trust categories (banking, insurance, health
   claims, legal) ⇒ level 1-2. Ordinary commerce chat about price/stock/
   shipping is NOT this case — Kak-chat is correct there even though money
   is involved; what's at stake in chat is the *order*, not a life
   decision.
2. **Business type** — corporate service suites write Anda with zero emoji;
   seller-run shops write Kak ([Qiscus vs Kitalulus disagreement, both
   real](https://www.qiscus.com/id/blog/contoh-kalimat-pesan-otomatis)).
3. **Message type inside one brand** — greetings can be Kak, product info
   Anda, order updates kamu, in the SAME shop ([Jubelio](https://jubelio.com/6-contoh-auto-reply-chat-shopee-bantu-tingkatkan-penjualan)).
   Register follows the message's job, not just the brand.
4. **Audience age + surface** — youth social tolerates 4; a marketplace
   store description stays kami-led 1-2 (real Shopee store descriptions
   use almost no Kak at all — [Golocad examples](https://golocad.com/id/insights/contoh-deskripsi-toko-shopee-yang-menarik)).

## The Kak rule (and the honest disagreement)

Marketplace convention: **address with Kak, write grammar with kamu** —
Kak is gender/age/status-neutral (traced to YouTubers' "Halo guys";
[Rubrik Bahasa/Kompas column](https://rubrikbahasa.wordpress.com/2024/07/23/kak-dan-kamu)).
BUT a Kompas-published linguist warns *kamu* to strangers violates tata
krama (especially for Javanese speakers — kamu maps to ngoko), and records
a real buyer losing interest when a seller's Kak slipped to kamu. Sellers
do it anyway; a ResearchGate study of real chat transactions shows
kak/kakak dominance ([study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373719753)).

**Guidance:** Kak + kamu is the market default and reads natural in chat.
In formal-adjacent contexts (first contact, high price, older audience),
hold Kak and re-check whether level 2 grammar is safer. Never *lo/gue*
toward a customer (level 5 is broadcast-only, never conversation).

## Never (any level)

- *kalian para pembaca / para calon pembeli* — nobody addresses readers
  this way.
- Register whiplash: Anda in the greeting, gaul two lines later.
- Slang at level 5 in anything with a price, a warranty, or a refund.
